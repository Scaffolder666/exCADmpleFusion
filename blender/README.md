# exCADmple — Blender design-reasoning player (9-step POC)

This is the **Blender-native proof of concept** for [exCADmple](../README.md):
explore a curated model's CAD operations, interaction cues and design reasoning
**while modeling your own object in the same 3D viewport**.

The current example is a synthetic, nine-step Phone Stand (45 seconds at 24 FPS).
Only *one* example is available today; multiple-example switching is part of
the intended research workflow, **not implemented yet**. This is not a
conversion of Fusion's or Blender's original modeling history.

## Intended interaction

A designer opens a **pre-authored model example** and uses exCADmple
to play through that model's preset design steps, CAD operation animations,
pointer trajectories, narration, transcript, and design rationale. Alongside
the reference, the user can freely edit their own design. **Reconstructing
steps from a newly imported or arbitrary model is out of scope for this
prototype**, not a prerequisite for evaluating the playback experience.

The included **synthetic Phone Stand demo** demonstrates:

- HiM-inspired profile-to-solid Extrude animations for base and lip, with an
  orange Sketch profile, moving golden 3D manipulator, distance indicator,
  and a separate illustrative support rotation;
- step navigation, play/pause, and native timeline scrubbing;
- a passive **ghost pointer dot** (soft amber circle, short fading trail,
  and click ripple) representing the *illustrative* previous designer's
  cursor activity, not moving or intercepting the learner's real cursor;
- an **example operation label** and step-aligned transcript/subtitles;
- nine sample English speech tracks (generated by `espeak`, **not
  recordings of a real designer**);
- a **right-edge HiM-inspired GPU-drawn viewport player** with a compact Why panel;
- cumulative modeling history: completed reference geometry **stays fully
  visible**; future features stay hidden; the active decision gets a
  highlight. The user's objects are never modified.

**Important distinction:** the ghost cursor and highlighted tool label are
a reenactment layer. They do not literally move Blender's system cursor,
click the Toolbar, or execute the original creator's input events.
The data is illustrative; it is not grounded in a real CAD session.

## Nine-step Phone Stand design example (v0.6)

This is now a **coherent evolving phone stand**, with **9 design decisions**
over 1080 frames (45 seconds at 24 FPS), rather than a disconnected feature
gallery or a three-step placeholder.

| Step | Modeling interaction | Design reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Establish the footprint | Sketch a wide profile; Extrude base | Establish stability before fixing other dimensions |
| 2. Choose a viewing angle | Form and rotate a back support (illustrative) | Balance visibility and an adjustable viewing angle |
| 3. Prevent slipping | Sketch and Extrude a retaining lip | Keep the phone seated without blocking user access |
| 4. Round base edges | Animate a native Bevel modifier | Improve handling without losing flat support |
| 5. Charging-cable passage | Move a live Boolean cutter into the lip | Preserve cable access while retaining two support surfaces |
| 6. Reinforce the back | Grow paired triangular ribs | Add stiffness locally rather than making the whole back heavier |
| 7. Pattern grip pads | Add four pads at footprint corners | Improve friction without expanding the footprint |
| 8. Lighten back support | Cut a central opening with a live Boolean | Trade material use against possible stiffness loss |
| 9. Check phone fit | Move a temporary phone proxy into position | Check clearance, charging access and viewing angle before final dimensions |

Each step has an authored first-person **Why**, three brief caption cues, a
scripted model-anchored ghost pointer and a synthetic narrated WAV file.
The geometry is progressively cumulative, and a temporary test phone is an
*auxiliary* reference, not part of the final stand.

**Fidelity boundary:** The two Extrudes and the later Bevel/Boolean stages
manipulate actual Blender geometry or modifiers. The initial support shaping,
paired-brace creation, patterning and phone placement are **pre-scripted
reenactments**, not replayed original editor commands. The speech, rationale
and cursor locations are illustrative, not historical recordings.

## Single-viewport HiM-style player (v0.5)

**Exactly one 3D Viewport. No split views, separate tutorial windows, or a
second Scene.** The pre-authored Phone Stand reference and the learner's own
model share that 3D space and one camera. The reference objects live in the
dedicated `exCADmple | Recorded example (read-only)` collection and have
`hide_select=True` so they cannot be accidentally selected or edited.

**Installation and workflow**

1. Enable the exCADmple add-on in Blender 4.5, open a 3D Viewport and press
   **N** to find the small exCADmple launcher in the Blender Sidebar.
2. Click **Open Phone Stand Example**. The HiM-inspired player should then
   appear directly **over the right edge of the same 3D Viewport**. If the
   modal input handler could not start automatically, click **Show HiM-style
   Player** in the small launcher.
3. Close the Blender **N Sidebar** to give the single modeling viewport more
   room. The floating player includes previous/next, Play/Pause, a
   within-step progress scrub bar, a short vertical **design-step timeline**,
   a compact *Why This Decision?* area, synchronized subtitle and speed.
4. Click within the player to control the recorded example; clicks outside
   it are passed through to **normal Blender modeling operations**.
   Create or edit your own geometry anywhere in the remaining viewport.
5. Use the **X** on the player overlay to close it. Reopen through the N
   Sidebar. The animation library's 20-operation gallery remains internal
   and is never shown as the learner's workflow.

### Ghost pointer: why a dot instead of a second mouse

The real Blender pointer remains fully under the learner's control. The
example pointer is **only GPU-drawn visual feedback**, never a real mouse
event or an OS cursor override. The pre-authored Phone Stand demo stores
`pointer_world` coordinates (a small ordered list of `at`, `xyz` and
`event: move/click/drag` samples). Each frame is interpolated in 3D and
projected through the user's active 3D viewport camera, so if the learner
orbits, pans or zooms, the dot stays associated with the **reference model**
instead of floating at an outdated screen coordinate.

The dot moves during a modeling step, leaves a short fading amber trail,
and briefly pulses at predefined click locations. The demo positions are
**synthetic, pre-scripted illustrations of cursor behavior** rather than
captured pointer recordings from a real designer. A true recorded example
could replace those samples in future, but recording/reconstructing CAD
history is not part of the present system scope.

Legacy developer examples can still use screen-relative `pointer` samples.
The visual marker has no mouse handler; only the separate HiM-style control
panel consumes clicks inside its own boundary. **Outside the control panel,
the learner's Blender mouse actions pass through normally.**

**Implementation:** `overlay_geometry.py` contains a pure, testable
right-edge panel layout and hit detection; `viewport_panel.py` handles
rounded GPU cards and compact typography; `EXCADMPLE_OT_viewport_panel`
is a modal operator that consumes mouse events *only within* the control
rectangle. All other events (including normal keyboard input) are passed
through to Blender. The existing ghost cursor renderer is retained.

**Known boundaries**

- Blender **headless CI tests** verify Python syntax, player data, screen
  layout/hit testing, operation animation and user/reference isolation.
  Actual GPU overlay visibility, mouse click handling, modal focus and the
  final appearance **must be verified on Blender desktop GUI**.
- Playback still advances the global Blender **Scene frame**. Speed buttons
  update the Scene frame rate; this also affects any animated user objects.
  Static user-authored geometry remains unchanged. An independent
  reference clock would be required if the learner animates their own model
  during example playback.
- The synthetic cursor is a visual reconstruction; it does not generate
  real Blender mouse events or click actual native tool buttons.
- This prototype loads a curated preset example, not arbitrary CAD files.

## Preset-model user workflow (earlier UI decisions; v0.6 keeps this scope)

The **only user-facing case at this stage** is the curated **Phone Stand
Example**. Users are not asked to select an operation from a gallery and
are not expected to load an arbitrary CAD model.

1. **Open the preset:** In Blender's 3D Viewport, press **N**, open
   **exCADmple**, and click **Open Phone Stand Example**. Alternatively,
   open a prepared `.blend` containing the example data.
2. **Browse this model's design steps:** Step navigation shows the modeling
   decisions in the **Phone Stand**, not a list of every available operation.
3. **Play, pause or scrub:** Each step's scripted geometry animation is
   synchronized with the simulated ghost cursor, operation label, optional
   sample narration, captions, and **Why This Decision?** explanation.
4. **Continue designing:** Model independently in the same scene. The add-on
   isolates its own reference collection. Users are free to follow, adapt,
   skip, or explore alternatives without formal mode-switching.

The interface prioritizes the **3D reference and a compact HiM-like player**
with previous/next, step-progress slider, relevant steps, the current
operation, and design reasoning. This is a **research prototype for exploring
a fixed, pre-authored example**, not a model-import or tutorial-generation
system.

### Internal HiM animation gallery (developer tests only)

The `him_ops.py` library contains a range of operation animations and the
`him_showcase.json` file is used by the **headless integration tests**.
No **20 Feature Animations** or generic **Import Example** control appears
in the normal exCADmple user interface. This catalog is not part of the
participant workflow. Operations are invoked by the preset model's steps.

The gallery currently covers twenty illustrative feature types. They are
**not claimed to be an exact one-to-one reproduction** of HiM Figure 7, and
some use explanatory transitions rather than exact CAD geometry algorithms.
It remains a test harness for future model-specific use.

### How each animation works

| Feature animation | Blender visual mechanism | Fidelity |
|---|---|---|
| Extrude | Explicit 2D profile, sketch tracing, moving extrusion top-face + gizmo | Parametric visual reenactment |
| Revolve | Screw Modifier, increasing angular sweep | Parameter-based geometry |
| Fillet | Bevel Modifier, increasing width, multiple segments | Approximate geometry parameter |
| Chamfer | Bevel Modifier, increasing width, one segment | Approximate geometry parameter |
| Circular Pattern | Position instances around the circle and grow into view | Animated occurrence placement |
| Rectangular Pattern | Position instances on a regular grid | Animated occurrence placement |
| Mirror | Reveal mirrored copy across a symmetry plane | Explanatory transition |
| Copy | Reveal and move a duplicate | Explanatory transition |
| Move | Position interpolation | Transform animation |
| Scale | Scale interpolation | Transform animation |
| Combine | Bring two overlapping solids together | **Visual metaphor, not Boolean union** |
| Split | Separate two resulting half-bodies | **Visual metaphor, not topology splitting** |
| Import | Reveal external-reference shape | Explanatory transition |
| Export | Move/diminish outgoing shape | Explanatory transition |
| Shell | Solidify Modifier increasing wall thickness | Parameter-driven shell preview |
| Draft | Shape Key deformation of selected upper vertices | Approximate taper |
| Sweep | Reveal curved tube along a path | Simplified sweep with circular section |
| Loft | Shape Key transitions between cross-sections | Simplified loft preview |
| Thread | Reveal helical curve/tube | Explanatory helix; not physical thread cut |
| Hole | Animated Boolean Difference cutter radius | Live Boolean preview |

**Important accuracy qualification:** HiM's paper reports support for 20
modeling features in its Figure 7. Its implementation text explicitly details
parameter animation for Extrude, Revolve, Fillet, Circular Pattern and
Rectangular Pattern, and fade/scale approaches for features including
Mirror, Split, Combine, Import and Export. This catalog covers those named
features and ten related feature types useful for design examples. It is
**not claimed to be a verified one-for-one reproduction of every label
in Figure 7** or of the original HiM renderers. In particular, Boolean
combination/splitting and actual selected-edge filleting remain incomplete.

### Technical structure

- `him_ops.py`: isolated Blender animation constructors and feature dispatcher.
- `him_showcase.json`: ordered illustrative operation specifications,
  with independent time ranges and simulated narration text.
- `smoke_him_catalog.py`: runs **real Blender** with every operation,
  checks animation values and evaluated geometry, and saves a `.blend`.
- `test_him_core.py`: pure validation for all 20 operations.

This gallery remains a *playback prototype*. The hardest next task is
extracting meaningful, author-grounded operation specs and geometry from
shared projects. The original source model is not altered.

## HiM-inspired operation animation (prototype implementation)

The Blender player now includes a deliberately **HiM-inspired feature animation**
for the two Extrude operations in the mock Phone Stand example. This replaces
the earlier "stretch a cube" illusion:

1. An orange **Sketch profile** is drawn using a real Blender Curve outline.
2. The tool phase switches from **SKETCH PROFILE** to **EXTRUDE TOOL**, then
   **DRAG EXTRUDE HANDLE**; the recorded ghost cursor remains synchronized.
3. The blue solid is generated from the polygon profile. Its **top vertices
   change position over time through Shape Keys**, keeping bottom vertices fixed,
   so the extrusion grows from the plane instead of scaling a cube.
4. A golden 3D **manipulator arrow** extends alongside the growing solid.
5. Playback presents the current extrusion distance, synchronized subtitles
   and mock audio; unrelated reference objects are de-emphasized. The
   user's own geometry is never changed.

The underlying `demo.json` stores an actual 2D polygon, its 3D origin and
an Extrude distance (in **Blender units**). For the sample:
```json
"animation": {
  "type": "extrude",
  "profile_xy": [[-1.7,-1.2],[1.7,-1.2],[1.7,1.2],[-1.7,1.2]],
  "origin_xyz": [-2.8,0,0],
  "distance": 0.26,
  "unit": "Blender units"
}
```
The second step still uses an illustrative growing/rotating support.

**Not yet implemented:** robust reconstruction of an arbitrary source
model's actual Sketch/Extrude history; curved profiles and inner loops;
Fillet/Revolve/Cut/Boolean live previews; highlighting Blender's actual
native Extrude menu button; moving the operating-system pointer; an
independent playback clock that leaves the user's scene animation frozen.
The yellow pointer and labeled tool phases are **graphical reenactments**,
not recovered original mouse events. The intro and UI visibly label the
data as synthetic.

The design choice follows Veuskens et al., *History in Motion* (SCF 2023):
animate the *operation parameter and its affected geometry* and keep the
original user's work untouched. Blender shape keys and Blender Curves
provide a simpler, in-canvas visual equivalent for explicit extrusion data.
It is **not** a reimplementation of HiM's full feature dependency filtering
algorithm nor its two-instance Fusion renderer.

The CI smoke test now confirms the profile mesh uses Shape Key deformation
instead of object scaling, checks the sketch trace and manipulator existence,
ensures only reference objects enter Focus Mode and leaves independent user
objects unchanged.

## Install the Blender POC (tested with Blender 4.5.15 LTS)

1. Download the ZIP add-on distributed with the demo, or zip just the
   `excadmple_blender` folder with its `__init__.py`, `core.py`,
   `demo.json`, and optional `audio/*.wav` files.
2. In Blender, open **Edit > Preferences > Add-ons > Install from Disk**
   (in newer versions, the same functionality may appear in Get Extensions).
   Select the ZIP, then enable **exCADmple — Design Reasoning Player**.
3. Open the **3D View**, press **N**, select the **exCADmple** tab.
4. Click **Open Phone Stand Example**. The HiM-inspired player should open
   **inside the existing 3D Viewport**. If needed, choose **Show HiM-style
   Player** from the small N-sidebar launcher.
5. Close the N Sidebar to free viewport space. Use the overlay's **Play/Pause**,
   previous/next, step cards, progress slider and speed buttons. The 3D
   reference, noninteractive ghost dot, captions and narration follow the
   same Scene time.
6. Add or edit a **separate user object** beside the reference to explore
   a technique while keeping your own geometry. The existing reference
   objects are marked unselectable.

When a WAV file is missing, the geometry and subtitles remain functional,
but the voice track is absent. For our CI demo we generate mock WAV tracks
with `espeak` before launching Blender. WAVs can also be supplied by a
creator in the same paths.

## Testing

Pure playback tests (no Blender required):

```sh
python -m unittest discover -s blender/tests -v
```

Native Blender headless integration:

```sh
blender --background --factory-startup \
  --python blender/scripts/smoke_blender.py
```

The native smoke test registers the add-on, constructs the **nine-step
cumulative reference (16 objects)**, adds nine narration strips, checks
Extrude/Bevel/Boolean, ribs/pads/fit proxy, reload/scene isolation, verifies
the original user mesh is untouched, and saves
`/tmp/excadmple_blender_smoke.blend`. It **cannot** verify that an N-panel
mouse click or GPU-drawn cursor looks correct in the interactive GUI;
that requires a real Blender viewport test.

## Architecture / scope

```text
excadmple_blender/core.py              Step data, captions, pointer interpolation
excadmple_blender/demo.json            Synthetic nine-step Phone Stand example
excadmple_blender/phone_stand_details.py  Bevel, Boolean, ribs, pads, fit check
excadmple_blender/procedural.py        Extrude shape-key animations
excadmple_blender/viewport_panel.py    In-viewport HiM-style GPU player
excadmple_blender/overlay_geometry.py  Layout and click hit-testing
excadmple_blender/__init__.py          Playback, collections, audio, ghost dot
scripts/smoke_blender.py               Real bpy headless integration
tests/                                 Pure Python validation tests
```

The example JSON is **versioned and explicitly restricted to**
`geometry_preset: "phone_stand_v1"` during this technology spike.
Importing an arbitrary JSON model does **not** yet reconstruct that
model. Loading an arbitrary .blend file alongside the reference is
possible, but extracting someone else's actual operation history is a
different, unsolved problem.

In a research system we would replace the preset with a format that
describes per-step scene changes plus tool/parameter samples, cursor
trajectories, synchronized original voice and transcript, and verified
author-supplied rationale. The same format could be played in several
CAD/3D editors; Blender is simply a more accessible first playback target.

We do not claim novel keyframe animation or cursor playback. The HCI
research question is whether **actively exploring examples in context**
supports a user's own design reasoning, choices, and adaptation.

## Known limitations

- Only a synthetic hardcoded demonstration model, not an actual F3D importer.
- The current nine-step preset uses **world-space ghost pointer samples**
  projected through the live camera, so the marker follows the reference
  when orbiting or zooming. Internal legacy test fixtures still support
  screen-relative pointer samples, which are not camera-anchored.
- The tool-name badge represents an operation; it does not highlight an
  actual Blender toolbar button.
- The native timeline currently drives reference animation as well as any
  other animated object in the scene. Users can still model freely, but
  independently animating their own scene would require a separate
  playback clock/scene or collection view layer.
- Audio playback is provided through Blender's Sequence Editor and may
  depend on the active audio device and playback settings.
- Previously completed reference geometry now remains solid as the example
  progresses; future reference steps remain hidden. This is not a full
  editable native Blender modeling history.
- The HiM-style GPU drawing and modal input event handling still require
  **hands-on Blender desktop validation**; headless tests don't verify clicks,
  text layout, or editing and playing simultaneously.
- There is no Example Library, no switching between multiple curated models,
  and no separate per-example seek positions yet.
- The reference shares the Blender Scene clock and FPS with learner objects.
  Playback may therefore also move or speed up the learner's own animated
  objects, even though their static geometry is not modified.
- Audio, rationale and pointer events are synthetic; speech and caption cues
  are step-timed, not synchronized word-by-word.
